Japanese has words that exist only to describe how food feels in your mouth ⚡

もちもち is chewy and springy, the mochi one, and it works for fresh bread too. サクサク is the light crispy sound, tempura or a good pastry. とろとろ is gooey and melty, melted cheese or a soft egg yolk.

These are not textbook adjectives, they are what everyone at the table actually says. Nobody here says delicious three times in a row. They tell you the texture instead, and it lands as a much better compliment to whoever cooked.

Try one at dinner and watch the table turn to look at you.
